## The short answer

By mid-2026, somewhere between 25% and 40% of all online queries
with commercial buying intent are happening on AI assistants —
ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and the long
tail of smaller assistants. The exact share varies by demographic,
by sector, and by query type. The direction is clear: it's a
material and growing fraction.

For a small business website, this changes the marketing maths.
Investing exclusively in traditional SEO when 30% of the funnel
has moved to a different surface is leaving money on the table.
Investing in AI search optimisation without abandoning traditional
SEO is the right balance for nearly every small business in 2026.

This piece is the grounded snapshot. Numbers where they exist.
Estimates where they don't. What the trajectory looks like for the
next 12-18 months.

## Where AI search has moved to

A rough breakdown of where commercial-intent queries are landing
in mid-2026:

| Channel | Share of commercial-intent queries | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search (blue links) | 45-55% | Declining |
| Google AI Overviews (atop search) | 10-15% | Growing |
| ChatGPT search | 10-15% | Growing |
| Perplexity | 5-8% | Growing |
| Claude | 4-7% | Growing |
| Other (Bing AI, Apple Intelligence, smaller) | 5-10% | Mixed |

These numbers are imprecise. They are reconstructed from publicly
reported Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity active-user
data, third-party traffic measurement (SimilarWeb, Cloudflare
Radar), and analyst estimates. The error bars are wide. The
direction is what matters.

The aggregate share of commercial queries handled by AI-assistant
interfaces (everything except pure blue-link Google search)
crossed the 30% mark in early 2026 and is on a trajectory to be
near 50% by mid-2027.

## What kinds of queries have moved fastest

Not all queries are migrating at the same rate. The pattern that
has emerged through 2025 and 2026:

### Queries that have moved heavily to AI assistants

- **"How do I" questions** — explainers, walkthroughs, comparisons. AI assistants are well-suited to these and users get better answers faster than Googling. 50%+ of these queries are now AI-first.
- **Product and service research** — "what's the best [thing] for [use case]". AI assistants synthesise comparisons more cleanly than a search result page. 40-50% AI-first.
- **Business and professional questions** — "what does [term] mean", "how does [process] work". 50%+ AI-first.
- **Complex, multi-part questions** — anything that would have required clicking through 3-5 search results to answer. 60%+ AI-first.

### Queries that have moved less

- **Navigational queries** — when the user knows the site they want and is just trying to get there. Still mostly direct Google search.
- **News and current events** — split. AI assistants handle these inconsistently.
- **Highly local queries** — "near me" queries. Mixed; Google Maps still strong but AI is catching up (see [local business AI search](/blog/local-business-ai-search/)).
- **Transactional queries** — when the user is ready to buy and just needs the link. Still mostly Google or direct.

### Queries where AI assistants are weak

- **Real-time information** — sports scores, stock prices, breaking news. Users go elsewhere.
- **Visual search** — searching by image. Specialised tools (Google Lens, etc.) still dominate.
- **Highly transactional buying journeys** — completing a purchase still typically lands on the retailer's site.

For a small business with a commercial website, the implication is
straightforward: the queries most likely to find your business
through "research and comparison" patterns are the queries most
likely to be running through AI assistants.

## Who's using AI search

The demographic split based on early 2026 data:

| Demographic | AI search adoption |
|---|---|
| Tech-adjacent professionals (SaaS, consulting, finance) | 60-70% use AI assistants weekly for work questions |
| Knowledge workers (general office workers) | 35-50% |
| SMB owners and decision-makers | 30-45% |
| General consumers under 35 | 35-50% |
| General consumers over 35 | 15-25% |
| Trade, manual, service-sector workers | 5-15% |

The adoption pattern correlates strongly with knowledge-work
density. The implication: if your business serves other businesses
(B2B), and particularly if you serve knowledge-intensive sectors,
your prospects are already on AI assistants more than half the
time when researching purchases.

If your business is consumer-facing and serves a broad demographic
(restaurants, trades, local services), the AI-search adoption is
more uneven, and the urgency is lower. Still real, but lower.

## What's changing this year

Five developments in 2026 worth being aware of:

### 1. Perplexity has become a meaningful traffic source

A year ago Perplexity was a curiosity. In mid-2026 it's the
fastest-growing AI search engine, with a citation pattern that's
particularly favourable to small businesses (more citations per
response, more diversity of sources, less concentration on the
top brands). Worth optimising for specifically.

### 2. Google AI Overviews are now in 40% of searches

Up from roughly 8% in early 2024. AI Overviews appear at the top
of Google search results for most informational queries, generated
from a small set of source URLs. Being one of the cited sources
in an AI Overview is now a meaningful goal in its own right.

### 3. ChatGPT search has become default

The default behaviour of ChatGPT is now to search the web for
queries it can't confidently answer from training. This was
opt-in a year ago. It's default now. The volume of queries
triggering live web searches has roughly tripled in twelve months.

### 4. The llms.txt standard has gained traction

A year ago it was a proposal. In mid-2026 multiple major AI
providers consult it, and a growing number of websites have
adopted it. Still under 10% of small business sites have one;
the early-mover advantage remains material.

### 5. Schema.org has become more important, not less

The expectation that AI assistants would obviate structured data
("the model will just read the page") has not panned out.
Structured data is more important in 2026 than it has ever been,
because the AI assistants depend on it for ground-truth claims.

## What still doesn't work

A few things that are pitched as relevant to AI search optimisation
but aren't moving the needle:

- **"Submitting" sites to AI assistants.** There is no submission interface to ChatGPT or Claude. Services selling "AI search submission" are scams.
- **AI-written content optimised by AI for AI.** The pattern of using AI to write content explicitly to rank in AI search hasn't worked. The signals that AI assistants weight (clarity, factual density, citation quality) are precisely the signals AI-generated marketing copy is bad at.
- **Tag stuffing and pseudo-schema.** Adding generic, off-topic schema to a site is detected and demoted. The same dynamic as Google's keyword-stuffing penalty, applied to structured data.
- **AI-generated FAQ sections with no substance.** Auto-generating "common questions and answers" with an LLM doesn't help if the answers are generic. The questions and answers need to be genuinely specific to your business.

## What still works

The boring, durable things:

- **Specific, factual, well-structured content.** Old advice. Still right. AI assistants reward exactly the same content that humans reward — they're just better at extracting it now.
- **Schema.org markup with depth and care.** Implemented properly, by someone who knows the spec.
- **Topical authority through clustered content.** Five well-linked, substantive pages on your speciality beats 50 shallow ones on random topics.
- **A clear, factual About page.** Disproportionately important. AI assistants consult it.
- **Real reviews and citations from credible third parties.** The trust signals that have always mattered, still matter.

## What to do in the next ninety days

If you're a small business owner reading this and have done none
of the AI-search work yet, the realistic priority list:

**Days 1-30: foundations.** Audit your site. Add `Organization` and (where applicable) `LocalBusiness` schema. Add an `llms.txt`. Add an FAQ section to the homepage with proper schema markup. Rewrite the About page for factual density.

**Days 30-60: depth.** Add `Service` schema for each service. Write a substantive new page on your core specialism. Implement topic clustering between your main pages.

**Days 60-90: content engine.** Publish three pieces of substantive content. Set up the basic AI-search tracking discipline (run five test queries through ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity monthly, log what changes).

By the end of the quarter, you'll be ahead of approximately 90%
of small business websites in your sector. The gap compounds.

## What the next twelve months look like

Predictions are cheap. A few that I'm comfortable making about the
next twelve months:

- **AI-search share of commercial queries will cross 50%.** By mid-2027. The trajectory is fairly firm.
- **`llms.txt` will become a baseline expectation.** The way `robots.txt` is today. Small businesses without one will be at a meaningful disadvantage by end of 2027.
- **Schema.org will get richer, not poorer.** New types and properties will land specifically for AI consumption (Speakable specifications already exist; expect more).
- **The template platforms will improve, but slowly.** Webflow first. Squarespace and Wix following at distance.
- **A class of "AI search optimisation" tools will mature.** Currently early-stage (Profound, Otterly, AI Rank). Expect three to five clear category leaders by end of 2027.
- **A correction in the AI-SEO services market.** Many of the current providers are selling decks rather than implementations. The market will reward the ones who actually deliver.

For a small business owner, the year ahead is about getting the
basics in place and then maintaining them. Not heroic. Just
durable, with a head start over competitors who delay.
