## The short answer

**Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of
structuring a website so that it appears in the responses
generated by AI search engines — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity,
Google AI Overviews, and the broader category of generative
search interfaces.

The term emerged in 2024 academic research and has been picked up
through 2025 and 2026 by the marketing community. It overlaps
significantly with what's also called **AEO (AI Engine
Optimisation)** and **LLM SEO**; the three terms are largely
interchangeable in practice.

What unites them: traditional Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)
optimised for ranking on Google's blue-link results. GEO/AEO/LLM
SEO optimises for being cited inside an AI-generated answer. The
mechanics are different. The deliverables are different. The
measurement is different.

This piece explains the differences and what small businesses
need to do.

## Where the term came from

The phrase "Generative Engine Optimization" was popularised in
academic work published in early 2024 by researchers at Princeton
and Stanford, who studied which characteristics of web content
predicted citation by generative search engines. They published a
practical taxonomy of techniques that improved citation rates —
adding statistics, using direct quotes, citing sources, structuring
content with clear headings.

The term resonated. Through 2024 and 2025 it became the working
shorthand for the broader practice. By 2026, "GEO" is the term you
will most often see in marketing literature; the alternatives
(AEO, LLM SEO, AI-search SEO) are mostly synonyms.

The thing being optimised for is concrete: when an AI assistant
generates a response that involves your topic, what predicts your
content being one of the cited sources?

## How GEO differs from traditional SEO

A side-by-side comparison clarifies what's similar and what's
genuinely new.

| Dimension | Traditional SEO | GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Rank on Google's first page of blue-link results | Be cited in an AI-generated response |
| **Primary signal** | Backlinks, keyword targeting, page authority | Content clarity, factual density, structured data |
| **Content style** | Optimised for keyword density and user dwell time | Optimised for direct factual answers and citability |
| **Page format** | Long-form articles with strategic keyword placement | Structured content with clear FAQ blocks, semantic markup, schema |
| **Measurement** | Keyword rankings, organic click-through, traffic | Citation appearance, AI-result tracking, response inclusion |
| **Time to result** | 6–12 months typical | 4–8 weeks typical |
| **Defensibility** | Slow to build, slow to lose | Faster to build, more dynamic |

A few of these deserve a closer look.

### Different signals

Traditional SEO's strongest signals are backlinks (other sites
linking to yours) and on-page keyword targeting. GEO's strongest
signals are content clarity (can a model extract a clean factual
claim?), structured data (does your page have schema.org markup
that pins down what the claim is?), and citability (does your
content read like something an AI would want to cite?).

These are not the same signal set. A site that does well on
traditional SEO often does badly on GEO, and vice versa.

### Different time horizons

Traditional SEO takes time. New content takes months to rank;
established sites lose ranking slowly. GEO is faster on both sides
— well-structured content can start being cited within weeks; old
content gets de-cited if newer, better-structured content replaces
it.

This is good news for small businesses with younger websites. The
"established players" advantage that traditional SEO entrenched is
weaker in GEO.

### Different measurement

You can track traditional SEO with rank-tracking tools and Google
Search Console. There isn't yet a clean equivalent for GEO. Some
tools (Profound, Otterly, AI Rank, the AI-specific tracking
modules being added to traditional SEO suites) are starting to
fill the gap. The practical baseline is manual: run your important
queries through ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, and see whether
your business appears in the responses.

## How GEO differs from AEO

The two terms are largely synonyms in practice. The distinctions
that some commentators draw:

- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** is sometimes used more broadly, covering both AI assistants AND zero-click search features like Google's featured snippets that predate generative AI.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** is sometimes used more narrowly, focused specifically on AI-generated responses (rather than retrieved snippets).

In practice the techniques are the same: structured content,
schema markup, FAQ blocks, factual clarity, `llms.txt`. If you do
GEO well, you do AEO well, and vice versa.

For a small business owner, the term doesn't matter. The
techniques do.

## What GEO requires

The practical work, ordered by impact:

### Structured data (schema.org)

The most important technical foundation. Schema.org markup is the
structured-data layer that AI engines read directly to understand
your business. For a small business website, the minimum set:

- `Organization` (your business as an entity)
- `LocalBusiness` (if you have a physical location)
- `Service` (each service you offer)
- `Person` (each member of your team you want findable)
- `FAQPage` (your most-asked questions, in structured form)

Implementation depth matters. Shallow schema (auto-generated by a
CMS, with minimal properties filled in) is barely useful.
Deep schema (manually written, with all relevant properties
populated and entity relationships explicit) is one of the strongest
single signals for GEO.

### Factual content density

GEO rewards content that contains specific factual claims. "We
help small businesses grow" is not a factual claim. "We are a
seven-person consultancy in Manchester that has helped 38
businesses in the manufacturing sector achieve ISO 9001 certification
since 2017" is.

Re-reading your existing content with this lens, and rewriting the
fluff into facts, is often the single change that produces the
largest GEO improvement.

### Clear semantic HTML

The AI's view of your page is text-only. If your headings are
proper h1 → h2 → h3 (rather than styled div tags), if your
content is in real paragraphs, if your lists are in actual ul or
ol tags, the AI can extract structure cleanly. Most platform-built
sites get this right; many heavily-styled bespoke sites get it
wrong.

### FAQ blocks

Question-and-answer formatted content is the single most cited
format in AI search responses. Adding an FAQ section to your key
pages — your homepage, your services pages, your About page — with
proper `FAQPage` schema markup, materially increases your citation
rate.

### `llms.txt`

The site-level summary file at the root of your domain. Covered in
detail in [the separate piece on llms.txt](/blog/what-is-llms-txt/).
Low cost, growing payoff as AI assistants increase reliance on it.

### Content depth and topic clustering

GEO rewards topical authority. Five well-structured pages on your
core specialism cited from each other and from a topic hub
out-perform thirty shallow pages on miscellaneous topics. This
takes time but compounds.

### Citation and supporting evidence

The Princeton/Stanford research found that content with
specifically cited sources, with statistics that have a clear
origin, and with direct quotes attributed to named individuals,
gets cited more often. Authority transfers. If your content cites
credible sources, AI assistants citing your content inherits some
of that credibility.

## What GEO is not

A few things that are sometimes pitched as GEO that aren't:

- **It's not a one-time fix.** Like SEO, GEO is ongoing. The model behaviours change. The standards evolve. The competitive set updates.
- **It's not the same as having an AI chatbot on your site.** Embedding ChatGPT on your contact page doesn't help your discoverability through AI search.
- **It's not the same as paying for AI advertising.** OpenAI and Anthropic don't sell ad placements in AI responses (as of mid-2026). GEO is the organic equivalent.
- **It's not a substitute for traditional SEO entirely.** Google still matters. The two practices overlap significantly. The smart move is to do both, with the GEO work being the higher-leverage of the two for most small businesses today.

## The competitive shape

A specific observation worth being explicit about: GEO competition
is much less crowded than SEO competition today. The reason is
historical — most agencies and consultants who built businesses on
SEO have not yet pivoted. The market for GEO services is much
smaller than the market for SEO services, in spite of the
underlying user demand being roughly comparable.

This means a small business that invests in GEO now is competing
against a smaller field. The wins are easier and they come faster.
This gap will close — probably by 2027 the market will look more
balanced — but in 2026 the window is genuinely open.

## What to do

If you run a small business and you want to start with GEO, the
practical sequence:

1. **Audit your current state.** Run five queries through ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity that should plausibly surface your business. See what comes up. Note which competitors are being cited and which are not.
2. **Implement the technical baseline.** Schema.org, `llms.txt`, FAQ blocks, clean semantic HTML. The first round of work takes one to two weeks if done properly.
3. **Re-do the content for factual density.** Re-read your homepage, your About page, and your top services pages. Rewrite the fluff into specifics. This takes another one to two weeks.
4. **Add depth.** Three to five substantive pieces of content on your core specialism, each cross-linked, each implementing the GEO basics. This takes two months at a sustainable pace.
5. **Track and iterate.** Re-run the AI queries quarterly. See what's changing. Adjust.

The first round of work delivers most of the immediate value. The
ongoing work is what compounds.
