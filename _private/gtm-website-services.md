## Executive summary

A new productised services line offering AI-discoverable websites
for small and medium professional services businesses. Positioned
as the technically credible alternative to template platforms
(Squarespace, Wix, Webflow) and to traditional web-design agencies,
on the basis that those competitors do not address the AI-search
discoverability layer that is becoming the dominant new traffic
source.

Initial target: three to four engagements in the first quarter,
ramping to eight to ten per quarter by month nine. Revenue target
at steady state: £80,000–£140,000 per year as a side practice
running alongside the existing senior architecture day job. Up to
£250,000+ per year as a primary practice if the day job changes.

The strategy explicitly avoids competing on price with template
platforms or on volume with offshore agencies. The competitive
position is "architecture-grade, AI-ready, built by a senior
engineer" — premium-mid, defensible, demonstrably differentiated
through both the work itself and the public body of writing that
sits behind it.

## Positioning statement

> "I build architecture-grade, AI-ready websites for professional
> services firms — sites engineered to be found by ChatGPT, Claude
> and Perplexity, not just by Google. Built by a senior architect,
> not assembled from a template."

The positioning works on three vectors:

| Vector | Position |
|---|---|
| Quality | Architecture-grade — engineered, not assembled. The same discipline applied to enterprise systems, applied to a small business website. |
| Differentiation | AI-discoverability as a first-class concern. Most competitors are not addressing this. The ones that are, are charging £15K+ for consulting decks rather than producing working sites. |
| Authority | Public proof: the personal site already implements every pattern being sold (schema.org, llms.txt, FAQ markup, topic hubs, structured data). Writing and case studies provide ongoing demonstration. |

What the positioning is NOT:

- Not a Squarespace/Wix/Webflow alternative on price.
- Not a freelance Fiverr or Upwork-tier offer.
- Not a "build me a website in 24 hours" speed play.
- Not a heavyweight enterprise vendor.

## Target customer

### Primary

Professional services SMBs in the 4–25 employee range. Specifically:

- Boutique consultancies (management, strategy, technology, specialist domains)
- Independent advisory firms (financial, legal, regulatory)
- Specialist B2B services (HR consultancies, learning and development providers, niche compliance specialists)
- Boutique creative agencies and design studios (when they recognise their own discoverability gap)
- Architecture and engineering practices (recursive but real — they know they're invisible)
- Accountancy practices and boutique law firms

The buyer is typically a partner, founder, or managing director.
Decision-making is concentrated. Budget is established and
accessible. The need is recognised once explained — but rarely
recognised before it is explained.

### Secondary

- Solo professional consultants (the "single-name advisory business") — smaller engagements but high willingness to act fast and refer.
- Other agencies looking to white-label the technical AI-discoverability work — steadier pipeline at lower margins.

### Explicitly out of scope (for now)

- E-commerce stores
- Membership / login / subscription sites
- Heavily custom or transactional applications
- Sites where the primary discoverability concern is paid acquisition rather than organic discovery
- Lead-generation sites that depend on aggressive ad spend rather than content depth

## Competitive landscape

| Category | Examples | Why I win against them |
|---|---|---|
| Template platforms with DIY discoverability | Squarespace, Wix, Webflow templates | Beautiful sites with no real AI-discoverability work underneath. Schema is shallow, no llms.txt, no FAQ structured data, no semantic HTML beyond the surface. |
| Cheap freelancers | Upwork, Fiverr, low-end UK freelancers | No quality story. No architectural rigour. No public authority signal. Buyer's choice if they want the cheapest option, not the right one. |
| Web design agencies (£3K–8K segment) | Local UK agencies, mid-tier digital | They sell design. They don't sell architecture. The AI-discoverability work, when offered, is bolted on rather than built in. |
| AI SEO consultants | Boutique consultancies pitching "AI SEO" decks | High price (£15K+) for advice, not deliverables. Many do not build sites; they hand recommendations to other agencies. I deliver the working site, not the deck. |
| Generative-AI-marketing firms | Larger marketing agencies adding AI offerings | Generally serving enterprise, not SMB. Pricing structure does not fit the SMB buyer. |

The gap I sit in: technically credible enough to charge premium,
small enough to serve SMBs, fast enough to ship in weeks rather
than quarters.

## Service offerings

Productised packages, three tiers plus retainer. Each is fixed-scope,
fixed-price, with a published deliverable.

### Audit — AI Discoverability Assessment (£499)

- Two-hour automated and manual audit of an existing site
- Written report covering schema.org markup, llms.txt presence, FAQ structure, semantic HTML, indexable content depth, AI-citation likelihood
- Prioritised list of fixes ranked by impact
- One 30-minute call to walk through findings

Purpose: lead magnet. Most audits convert into a Build or Upgrade
engagement. The audit itself is profitable as a standalone but the
business value is the qualified pipeline it generates.

### Upgrade — AI Discoverability Retrofit (£2,495)

- Apply the audit findings to the customer's existing site
- Add schema.org Person, Organisation, Article, FAQ, Service markup
- Create and deploy llms.txt
- Add canonical URLs and sitemap
- Restructure key pages for AI-readability
- Two weeks turnaround

Purpose: low-friction entry point for customers not ready to rebuild.

### Build — AI-Ready Website (£4,995)

- Custom-built 5–8 page brochure site
- Full AI-discoverability stack from day one
- Schema.org, llms.txt, FAQ markup, semantic HTML
- Custom design (not template-based)
- Cloudflare hosting setup
- Basic on-page SEO
- One round of content review and refinement
- 30-day post-launch support
- Four to six weeks turnaround

Purpose: the core offering. Most engagements land here.

### Authority — Build plus Content Engine (£9,995)

- Everything in the Build tier, plus:
- Blog setup with proper categorisation, RSS feed, topic hubs
- Initial content strategy and topic map
- Five seed blog posts written and published
- Topic-cluster cross-linking discipline established
- Three-month launch-period support

Purpose: the package buyers ready to invest in authority-building.
Higher margin, deeper engagement, foundation for retainer conversion.

### Retainer — Authority Maintenance (£1,200/month)

- Monthly AI-result monitoring against tracked queries
- Schema and structured-data tuning
- Two blog posts per month (1,200–1,800 words each)
- Quarterly content audits
- Annual technical audit
- Slack or email channel for ongoing queries

Purpose: recurring revenue, sustained relationship, opportunity for
upsell into advisory work.

## Pricing rationale

Pricing tested against three benchmarks:

| Benchmark | Where the offer sits |
|---|---|
| Template platforms (DIY, £200/year) | Significantly higher, but for a fundamentally different deliverable. Buyers in this segment are not the target. |
| UK mid-tier web design agencies (£3K–8K) | Aligned. The Build tier at £4,995 sits in the middle of this range, where buyers are most comfortable. |
| AI SEO consultants (£15K+) | Substantially lower. The differentiator is that the offer includes the implementation, not just the advice. |
| Senior freelance developer day rate (£600–900/day in UK) | The Build package represents approximately 7–8 days of work at a comfortable margin. The Authority package, 14–16 days. Sustainable. |

The pricing is also designed to be quotable without negotiation. The
Audit is a fixed sub-£500 transaction. The Build is below the £5K
threshold above which many SMB buyers require additional approval.
The Authority is below £10K — the same threshold at the next tier.

## Marketing strategy

The marketing strategy is the content strategy. Twelve blog posts
in the first quarter, eight per quarter thereafter. Topic clusters
that mirror the search-intent map above.

The posts are not generic AI-SEO commentary. They are practical,
specific, and weighted toward the actions a small business owner
can take this week. Each one positions the author as a credible
technical practitioner — not as a marketer.

A separate /services/ page launches on the personal site by week
six, with the productised packages and pricing. The page lives at
`tarun.bulchandanis.com/services/` initially. Conversion path:
blog post → /services/ → contact form (or LinkedIn DM) →
discovery call → engagement.

Lead capture: a simple email signup on the blog for "AI search
updates for small businesses" — quarterly digest of what is
changing in the space, with a soft service mention. Builds a list
asset over time.

## Operational model

Capacity assumptions:

- Build engagement: 7–8 working days at 6 productive hours/day = 45–50 hours
- Authority engagement: 14–16 working days = 90–100 hours
- Upgrade engagement: 2–3 working days = 12–18 hours
- Audit engagement: 4 hours (delivered weekly in a batch)
- Retainer: 6–8 hours per month per client

At a target of 12 hours per week dedicated to this practice (side
project alongside day job), realistic capacity is approximately:

- 1 Build per month, plus
- 1–2 Upgrades per month, plus
- 2–3 Audits per month, plus
- 2–3 retainer clients ongoing

Total annual revenue at that cadence:
- 12 Builds × £4,995 = £59,940
- 18 Upgrades × £2,495 = £44,910
- 30 Audits × £499 = £14,970
- 3 Retainers × £1,200 × 12 = £43,200

Total potential: ~£163,000 per year as a side practice with this
cadence. Realistic in-year-one target after sales-ramp: ~£60,000–
£80,000.

At full-time capacity (40 hours per week), capacity doubles and
the revenue ceiling sits around £325,000–£350,000 per year before
any team expansion.

Operations:

- All client work executed from the existing tooling stack (Cursor, Claude Code, the personal-site infrastructure as the showcase)
- Project management via Linear or a similar lightweight tool
- Client communication via a dedicated work email + Slack Connect for active engagements
- Contracts handled via simple fixed-scope agreements (£499/£2,495/£4,995/£9,995 pricing means heavy contracts are not required)
- Invoicing via FreshBooks or similar
- Tax: separate sole-trader account if running as a side practice; consideration of Ltd if revenue passes £85K threshold

## Risks and mitigations

### Risk: the AI-search opportunity does not develop as projected

AI search adoption among SMB customers is real but moves slowly. If
the underlying market does not develop, the positioning weakens.

Mitigation: the work also produces excellent traditional SEO sites.
The "AI-discoverable" framing wraps a technically sound underlying
deliverable. Buyers get a good site either way.

### Risk: template platforms close the gap

Squarespace and Webflow could ship AI-discoverability features that
collapse the differentiation.

Mitigation: the differentiation is not just about features, it is
about depth and customisation. Templates will close 80% of the gap;
the remaining 20% is where the buyers who care about authority
content live. That segment is the actual target.

### Risk: day-job constraints

Running a parallel commercial practice from a full-time senior
architecture role has both contractual and time constraints. The
employment contract may have non-compete or external-engagement
clauses.

Mitigation: review contract terms before launching publicly.
Consider scope limitations (e.g. avoid clients in the renewables
sector or directly competing industries). Disclose to the employer
where appropriate. Time-box delivery to evenings and weekends
strictly.

### Risk: scaling beyond solo

If demand exceeds solo capacity, the temptation is to hire. This
introduces operational complexity and dilutes the "built by a senior
engineer" proposition.

Mitigation: maintain a deliberate cap on engagements. Refer excess
demand to a network of trusted collaborators rather than hiring.
Re-evaluate capacity quarterly.

### Risk: client churn or dissatisfaction

A single dissatisfied client at this small scale could damage the
authority signal substantially.

Mitigation: rigorous engagement scoping. Clear deliverables. Money-
back guarantee on the Audit tier. Weekly check-ins during Build
engagements. Post-engagement testimonial collection.
